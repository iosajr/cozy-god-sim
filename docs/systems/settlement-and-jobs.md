# Settlement & Jobs

The settlement as a real thing, and the coordinator that hands out work.

## Decided

- A settlement owns its members, its stores, and its buildings. Its
  shortages create the available work.
- The job manager is its own module, holding the universal jobs every
  being does wherever it lives — eat, sleep — and taking settlement
  information as input.
- Beings hold their own references. A villager knows its home; a house
  knows its occupants.
- Coordination lives here so beings never negotiate with each other.
  Anything needing two beings to agree goes through the settlement.
- Work is offered, never assigned. A being asks and chooses.
- A settlement without a village still needs tasking — a lone being gets
  its own pool of one, rather than being forced to count as a settlement.

## Buildings

- Beings decide to build. There is no player build menu or placement UI.
- **Shelter is not housing.** Shelter is the baseline survival need and
  can be satisfied by something as minimal as a nearby tree. Housing is a
  constructed upgrade, and is not required to survive.
- A house needs a real position, and a placeholder model is enough.
- Building a house requires wood, and requires a real need — a
  population outgrowing its housing — not a flat one-house-per-N rule.

## The store is a place, and it progresses

Where a settlement's food sits depends on how far along it is: a spot on
the ground at first, a house once housing exists, a dedicated storehouse
later. How a settlement moves between those tiers is not designed.

## Farm

- A cycle: seed, grow, harvest, re-seed. Seeding is a real act — a farm
  sits idle until a being walks over and plants it.
- Growing needs periodic watering, not constant attendance. Water comes
  from rain, from a being carrying it, or from a river.
- Harvest is autonomous and costs trips. A being carries a limited
  amount, so a large harvest takes several journeys.
- **A farm ready to harvest supports several workers at once**, sharing
  one drain on what remains. This is what stops everyone swarming a
  single patch — not a one-worker lock.

## Open

- Is a solitary being with only their own shelter a settlement of one,
  or not a settlement at all? Settlement itself may need reworking once
  tree and animal beings are in play, since those want their own kinds —
  burrows, dens, groves.
- What a settlement lacking food or shelter actually suffers. It should
  eventually matter.
- Where wood comes from. Nothing produces it.
- What a river means for watering a farm.
