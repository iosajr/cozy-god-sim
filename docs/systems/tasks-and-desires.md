# Tasks & Desires

What a being decides to do, and doing it.

## Decided

- A task is a whole job — sleep, eat, harvest that field — never a single
  action. Sleep is find shelter, go there, rest.
- Steps are shared between tasks. Going somewhere and waiting a while are
  written once and reused, rather than each task carrying its own travel.
- **Travel is part of every task, including at the settlement.** A being
  must physically reach food to eat it, however close the food is.
- A being picks its target from what it knows, not from a search of the
  world.

## Priority is a number

- A task carries a numeric urgency score, not a fixed category. Urgency
  rising is recomputing a number.
- **Must-do, Important and Passtime are vocabulary for ranges of that
  number**, not stored fields. A rule asks whether a score clears the
  must-do threshold; it never reads a band.
  - Must-do is genuinely life-threatening: about to die of hunger or
    exhaustion, or another being in mortal danger this one could help.
  - Important is scheduled needs and work that matters but is not an
    emergency.
  - Passtime is optional filler — gathering, harvesting, exploring,
    herding.
- **A task's band moves with context.** Gathering food climbs out of
  passtime when the settlement is critically short.

## Asking, not assigning

- A being asks for work at real decision points: its task finished, it
  was interrupted, or its own periodic tick fired. Nothing re-scores every
  being every tick, so cost tracks how many beings need a decision, not
  how many exist.
- **No task queue.** A pending need is not stored in a list. The current
  task finishes, the being asks again, and the need — which never went
  away — surfaces then.

## Interruption

A heuristic, deliberately not a precise algorithm. A task close to
finishing, or one where stopping halfway causes a bad outcome, is
generally allowed to finish — a being nearly done bringing the sheep back
should not drop them to make bedtime. Genuine must-do emergencies
interrupt regardless.

## Needs

- **Checked periodically, roughly once or twice a day. Not a continuously
  depleting meter.**
- Hunger runs fine, hungry, starving. Tiredness runs fine, tired,
  exhausted. Both are one progression covering every way of failing to
  meet the need, not separate outcomes per cause.
- Eating is schedule-driven, about twice a day.
- Sleep fires at nightfall and lasts about eight hours. **It looks
  ahead**: a being works backward from the target sleep time, subtracts
  how long the walk home takes, and leaves early enough to be asleep by
  nightfall rather than only then setting off.
- Being at the settlement with food in the store is the easy case. Being
  away having brought provisions is the second. Being away without them
  means foraging or hunting for real, with real risk.

## Getting food

Two levers, deliberately different. **Farming is a reliable slow burn** —
the cycle takes its time regardless of how urgent things are. **Hunting is
a faster payoff and explicitly not something to rely on.**

A low store does not yank anyone off what they are doing. It raises the
priority of food work, so gathering is more likely to win the next time
someone asks.

## Idle

A real task, not a placeholder: wandering interlaced with standing still.
